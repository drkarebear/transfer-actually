Two systems, different shortcuts

CSU and UC offer different ways to make the path more predictable.

These are useful only when they match your situation, so do not assume every student needs every program.

CSU

If you know the major but not the campus

  • Check whether your major has an ADT. An AA-T/AS-T can provide CSU transfer benefits when the degree and CSU program align.
  • Use the CSU Transfer Planner to explore campuses, majors, requirements, and progress.
  • Check impaction. High-demand campuses or programs may use additional admission standards.
  • If you are a new CCC student, check TSP eligibility early. TSP is a separate dual-admission pathway that can guarantee a particular CSU campus and program when its agreement requirements are met.
Fall 2026 note: CSU currently lists the Transfer Success Pathway agreement cycle as August 1–October 31 for eligible students beginning at a CCC this fall. Always verify the current cycle before acting.
UC

If you know the major but not the campus

  • Check UC Transfer Pathways. For selected majors, one pathway provides a common set of lower-division major-preparation courses across UCs offering that major.
  • Consider Pathways+. It combines a Transfer Pathway with a TAG option when the major and campus are eligible.
  • Use UC TAP to organize coursework and, when eligible, submit a TAG.
  • Use ASSIST when you narrow to individual campuses or when the major is not covered by a Transfer Pathway.
One useful difference: a UC Transfer Pathway helps standardize major preparation across UC campuses; it does not mean every campus has identical admission selectivity.

Avoid the expensive mistakes

Four planning habits that can create unnecessary units or unpleasant surprises.

“I will finish all my GE first, then think about my major.”

That can be risky in majors with long prerequisite sequences.

Better: look at major preparation early, then fit GE around the sequence.

“The major has the same name, so the requirements must be the same.”

Major preparation can vary by campus, school, and program.

Better: compare the actual current agreements.

“I found one dream school. I only need to plan for that one.”

Selective transfer admission can make a one-campus plan fragile.

Better: build a small shortlist with more than one viable destination.

“An ADT means I have handled UC too.”

The statewide ADT guarantee is a CSU pathway, not a blanket UC guarantee.

Better: treat CSU and UC pathways as related but distinct.

Bring this to counseling

Do the research first. Then ask better questions.

You do not have to arrive knowing the answers. Arrive knowing what needs to be checked.

My shortlist

Write down 3–5 destinations.

  • University + campus
  • Exact major/program name
  • Application term you are aiming for
  • Whether the program is selective or impacted
Major preparation

Notice the overlap and the differences.

  • Courses required everywhere
  • Courses required only at some destinations
  • Recommended courses
  • Sequences and prerequisite chains
Pathway

Ask which structured route fits.

  • ADT / similar CSU major?
  • TSP eligible and still within the agreement window?
  • UC Transfer Pathway or Pathways+?
  • TAG eligible?
Questions

Bring the uncertainty with you.

  1. Which of these destinations fit the coursework I am already taking?
  2. Am I missing an early prerequisite that could delay later courses?
  3. Is my GE plan helping—or crowding out major preparation?
  4. Which requirements need a campus-specific check?
  5. What is my backup plan if my first-choice program is highly selective?
