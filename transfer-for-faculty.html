A 10-minute faculty orientation

Transfer for Faculty, Actually.

You do not need to memorize every transfer rule. You do need a mental model for how courses move through the system, how requirements differ, what statewide structures actually mean, and when a faculty answer needs a current check.

The whole system in one line

A course can play several different roles before it becomes part of an individual student's transfer plan.

Course → Transferability → Articulation → GE / Major Prep → Transfer Pathway → Student Plan

The mental model

Six layers. Different questions.

Most transfer confusion comes from treating several different questions as if they all mean “Does this class count?”

1

Course

What is actually approved in the local course outline?

2

Transferability

Will a receiving system award transfer unit credit?

3

Articulation

Has an official relationship been established to a course or requirement?

4

Requirement

Does it satisfy GE, major preparation, or another program requirement?

5

Pathway

Is it part of an ADT, TAG/TSP, Transfer Pathway, or other structured route?

6

Student plan

Given this student's record, destination, major, and timing, should they take it?

Faculty shortcut: you can often explain layers 1–5 conceptually. Layer 6 is where a complete record and individualized planning usually become essential.

Four concepts worth getting exactly right

These pairs are related. They are not interchangeable.

Transferable

The receiving system awards transfer unit credit for the course.

It does not automatically tell you whether the course satisfies GE, a major requirement, or a specific university course.

Articulated

An official agreement identifies how coursework at one institution can satisfy coursework or a requirement at another.

Articulation is more specific than “this course transfers.”

General education

Broad lower-division learning requirements. For many current CCC students planning CSU/UC transfer, Cal-GETC is the statewide intersegmental GE pattern.

Finishing GE does not necessarily finish preparation for the major.

Major preparation

Lower-division courses expected for the specific major/program at the destination institution.

The same major name can have different preparation across campuses.

The alphabet soup

ASSIST, ADT, C-ID, and CCN do different jobs.

You do not need every acronym. You do need to know what question each one can and cannot answer.

ASSIST: “What is officially articulated or transferable?”

ASSIST is the official statewide repository of articulation and transferability information. It shows how CCC coursework may be used for elective credit, GE, and major requirements at CSU, UC, and participating AICCU institutions.

Faculty use: look at the correct academic year, institution pair, and agreement type before making a claim about a particular course or major.

Practice in ASSIST, Actually.

ADT: “Is there a structured CCC → CSU degree pathway?”

An AA-T or AS-T is a California Community College Associate Degree for Transfer. Eligible students receive CSU-system priority-admission benefits, but the statewide guarantee is not automatically a promise of one specific campus or major.

When a student transfers into a CSU program deemed similar to the ADT, the degree includes important unit-completion protections, subject to the CSU's conditions.

Review ADT, Actually.

C-ID: “Does this course match an intersegmental descriptor?”

C-ID is the Course Identification Numbering System. Intersegmental faculty develop course descriptors that define the minimum course elements needed for comparability; local courses can be reviewed against those descriptors.

Faculty use: C-ID is especially visible in ADT/TMC work and statewide course comparability. A C-ID designation is not a substitute for reading the actual articulation agreement when the question is about a particular university requirement.

CCN: “Why does this CCC course now have the same number statewide?”

Common Course Numbering (CCN) is the newer student-facing statewide system intended to give comparable CCC courses the same course number and improve credit mobility and transfer clarity.

CCN templates standardize specified parts of the course outline for participating courses. The Chancellor's Office also explicitly tells curriculum committees to work with articulation when adding local content because changes can affect course-to-course and major-preparation articulation.

So CCN and C-ID are not the same?

Correct. They are connected, but they are not interchangeable.

CCN is a student-facing common numbering and course-template initiative across the CCC system. C-ID is a descriptor-based course comparability system used in transfer/articulation and ADT/TMC infrastructure. Some CCN courses are aligned with existing C-ID descriptors, which is one reason the acronyms can appear together.

What changes by destination?

The statewide structures help. The destination still matters.

A faculty member can understand the statewide framework and still need to verify the receiving institution's current program information.

CSU Think ADT + impaction + major preparation.

CSU has structured ADT and TSP pathways, but campus/program availability and selectivity still matter.

UC Think major preparation early.

UC emphasizes major preparation in transfer review; UC guidance specifically warns that extensive major prep can deserve priority over finishing all GE first.

Independent / other Do not import CSU/UC assumptions.

Participating AICCU institutions may publish agreements in ASSIST; other private or out-of-state destinations may use their own transfer-credit and admission systems.

Why faculty should care beyond advising questions

A curriculum change can ripple into transfer.

This is where faculty transfer literacy becomes especially important for chairs, curriculum authors, and discipline faculty.

One course revision can touch…

Course outline C-ID / CCN alignment Articulation + major prep ADT / program pathway

Before assuming a change is transfer-neutral

  • Check whether the course currently has CSU/UC transferability or GE approval.
  • Check whether it appears in course-to-course or major-preparation articulation.
  • Check whether it carries a C-ID designation or is governed by a CCN template.
  • Check whether it sits inside an ADT/TMC or other transfer program.
  • Bring the articulation officer in early rather than after local curriculum approval.

Current CCCO guidance is explicit: curriculum committees should work with articulation on common-numbered courses and consider whether local additions to the COR could affect course-to-course and major-preparation articulation.

Who owns the answer?

Transfer is shared work, not one person's job.

Faculty

Course content, discipline expectations, curriculum design, explaining general concepts, and recognizing when a student question needs verification.

Counseling

Whole education plans, sequencing, transcript-wide decisions, GE + major integration, and individualized transfer planning.

Articulation officer

Institutional articulation, ASSIST agreements, C-ID submissions/coordination, transfer implications of curriculum, and intersegmental articulation work.

Receiving institution

Admission criteria, selection, impaction, program-specific requirements, conditions of admission, transfer-credit decisions it owns, and campus policy.

The goal is not “stay in your lane.” The goal is to collaborate without making authoritative claims outside the information you actually have.

The cheat sheet

If you remember only six things…

1. Transferable ≠ articulated.

Unit credit and requirement equivalency are different questions.

2. GE ≠ major prep.

A student can finish GE and still be missing critical preparation.

3. ASSIST is where you verify articulation.

Use the right year, institutions, and agreement type.

4. ADT ≠ guaranteed favorite CSU.

The statewide guarantee has conditions and does not automatically promise a particular campus or major.

5. CCN ≠ C-ID.

Connected statewide structures, different functions.

6. Curriculum can affect transfer.

Bring articulation into transfer-relevant revisions early.
