Faculty transfer help in the moment

A Student Just Asked Me…

You do not need to become a transfer counselor to be useful. You need enough transfer literacy to explain the concept, recognize what must be checked, and help the student reach the right next source without confidently guessing.

Use the same four-part response every time.
1. What can I explain? 2. What needs a current check? 3. Who owns the individualized answer? 4. What should I not guess?

The faculty boundary

Explain the system. Do not invent the student's plan.

Faculty expertise is especially useful for concepts, curriculum, disciplines, and helping students interpret what they are seeing. Individual transfer planning can depend on the complete record, destination, term, catalog context, and current institutional rules.

🟢 You can explain

Transferability vs. articulation, GE vs. major prep, what ASSIST is, what an ADT generally does, and why destination matters.

🔎 Check before answering

A particular campus, major, academic year, course articulation, similar-major status, impaction, or current admissions rule.

👤 Refer individualized planning

Transcript-wide decisions, course sequencing, repeats, unit totals, catalog rights, eligibility, admission conditions, or financial aid.

⛔ Do not guess

Whether a student “will get in,” whether one course substitution is safe, or whether a change cannot affect an offer of admission.

Real questions

What did the student ask?

Filter by the kind of question you are dealing with, or scan them all.

Course counting

“Will your class transfer to UCLA?”

🟢 Explain

A course can be UC-transferable for unit credit without being articulated to a specific UCLA course or satisfying this student's major preparation.

🔎 Verify

Check the current ASSIST academic year, the CCC's UC-transferable course list, and the relevant UCLA agreement if the question is about a particular requirement.

👤 Refer

If the student is deciding whether this course belongs in the full education plan, counseling should review the destination and major with the complete record.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not turn “UC transferable” into “this satisfies the UCLA requirement you need.”

A useful response

“It may transfer for unit credit, but let's separate that from whether it satisfies something specific at UCLA. ASSIST is where we can verify that part.”

Course counting

“Is this class UC transferable?”

🟢 Explain

“UC transferable” means UC has approved the course for transfer unit credit. It does not automatically mean GE, major preparation, or course-to-course articulation.

🔎 Verify

Use ASSIST's current UC Transferable Courses list for the student's CCC and the correct academic year.

👤 Refer

If the real question is “Should I take it?”, the answer belongs inside the student's complete transfer plan.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not rely on an old syllabus, memory, or “it has always transferred.”

A useful response

“We can verify whether UC gives it transfer unit credit. Then we still need a separate check if you're asking whether it fills GE or your major.”

Major preparation

“Do I need this class for my major?”

🟢 Explain

Major preparation is destination-specific. The same major name can have different lower-division preparation at different universities.

🔎 Verify

Use the current major agreement in ASSIST and the receiving campus's program/admissions information.

👤 Refer

Counseling should help prioritize courses when several campuses, prerequisites, GE, and timeline constraints compete.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not answer from the discipline name alone or from what one university requires.

A useful response

“Maybe—but the university and exact program decide that. Let's first identify where you want to transfer.”

GE + major

“I finished Cal-GETC. Am I ready to transfer?”

🟢 Explain

Cal-GETC addresses lower-division general education. Transfer preparation can also include major preparation, minimum admission requirements, units/GPA, and campus/program selection criteria.

🔎 Verify

Check the intended university and major, current admission requirements, and major preparation.

👤 Refer

Counseling should verify whether the student's overall transfer plan is complete.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not equate “GE complete” with “transfer-ready for this major.”

A useful response

“That's a big piece of the puzzle. Now we need to make sure your major prep and admission requirements are lined up too.”

ADT

“I have an ADT. Am I guaranteed into CSUN?”

🟢 Explain

The statewide ADT benefit is priority admission to the CSU system for eligible students; it is not, by itself, a guarantee of one specific campus or major.

🔎 Verify

Check whether the CSU program is deemed similar to the ADT, current campus/major admission conditions, and any impaction.

👤 Refer

Counseling/admissions can help the student apply the ADT to the actual campus/major plan.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not translate “Degree with a Guarantee” into a promise of the student's chosen campus.

A useful response

“Your ADT gives you important CSU transfer benefits, but the statewide guarantee does not mean automatic admission to CSUN specifically. Let's verify the similar-major and campus details.”

ASSIST language

“ASSIST says ‘no course articulated.’ Does that mean my class doesn't transfer?”

🟢 Explain

No established articulation for that item is not the same statement as “this course is nontransferable.” Transferability and articulation answer different questions.

🔎 Verify

Check the appropriate transferability list plus the complete agreement and its notes for the correct year.

👤 Refer

If the missing articulation changes the student's major plan, counseling and/or the articulation officer can help identify what the agreement means in context.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not invent an equivalency that ASSIST does not show.

A useful response

“That means ASSIST is not showing an established articulation for that item. Let's not turn that into a broader claim about whether your course transfers at all.”

Changed major

“I changed my major. Did I waste all these classes?”

🟢 Explain

A course can still serve transfer credit, GE, another requirement, or elective units even if it no longer serves the new major. The old and new pathways need to be compared.

🔎 Verify

Recheck the new major's preparation, the destination, GE, and any structured pathway such as ADT or TAG.

👤 Refer

This is exactly the kind of transcript-wide replanning counseling should do with the student.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not call courses “wasted” or promise that everything will apply to the new degree.

A useful response

“Not necessarily. Let's separate what those courses still do from what the new major requires. Your counselor can help rebuild the plan without assuming you start over.”

Grade / W

“I got a W. Did I ruin my transfer?”

🟢 Explain

There is no useful one-word answer. The consequence depends on what course it was, the larger record, whether a required sequence changed, and the rules of the destination/program.

🔎 Verify

Check whether the course is required for eligibility, major preparation, GE, or a transfer pathway and whether the student's timeline changed.

👤 Refer

Counseling should review the whole record; after application or admission, the receiving campus may also need an update.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not reassure with “Ws don't matter” or alarm with “a W will prevent transfer.”

A useful response

“A W doesn't tell me enough to predict your transfer outcome. Let's figure out what role that course played and where you are in the application process.”

Unit total

“I have too many units. Can I still transfer?”

🟢 Explain

A raw total is not enough information. Receiving institutions may treat CCC transferable units, nontransferable coursework, and four-year/upper-division coursework differently.

🔎 Verify

The student's complete record and the receiving institution's current unit policies need review.

👤 Refer

Send this to counseling and, when needed, the receiving university's admissions/evaluation office.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not add units aloud from the student's memory and pronounce them eligible or ineligible.

A useful response

“The total alone doesn't answer that. We need to know what kind of units they are and how your destination treats them.”

Course choice

“Should I take your class or another GE class?”

🟢 Explain

You can explain what your course covers, whether it is currently approved for transfer/GE when verified, and how it fits the discipline.

🔎 Verify

Check the student's intended pathway and destination before treating two “GE-looking” choices as interchangeable.

👤 Refer

The choice belongs in the student's whole education plan, especially if one option also serves major preparation or a prerequisite.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not choose a course for the student based only on which one seems easier, more interesting, or broadly transferable.

A useful response

“I can tell you exactly what my course does. Before I tell you it's the better choice, let's make sure it fits the transfer plan you're actually following.”

After transfer

“Can I just change majors after I transfer?”

🟢 Explain

Change-of-major rules are set by the receiving institution and can be especially restrictive for selective, capped, impacted, or highly sequenced programs.

🔎 Verify

Use the actual university/program's rules for transfer students, not a general assumption about changing majors.

👤 Refer

Admissions or the receiving program should answer whether the intended switch is possible.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not advise students to apply to an easier major as a back door unless the institution explicitly supports the later change.

A useful response

“Maybe, but that's a university-specific policy—not a safe transfer strategy to assume. Let's check the rule before you build a plan around changing later.”

Conflicting advice

“My counselor told me something different. Who is right?”

🟢 Explain

Transfer answers can differ because people are answering different questions, using different academic years, or discussing different campuses/majors. A conflict is a cue to locate the authoritative source—not to create a faculty-versus-counselor contest.

🔎 Verify

Identify the exact claim, destination, major, term/year, and source. Check ASSIST or the institution that owns the policy.

👤 Refer

Bring the source back to the counselor; involve the articulation officer when the dispute is specifically about articulation.

⛔ Do not guess

Do not tell the student the counselor is wrong based on a rule you remember from another campus or year.

A useful response

“Let's make the disagreement specific. What exact requirement are we talking about, for which campus and major, and what current source answers it?”

Three sentences worth memorizing

You do not need a perfect answer on the spot.

These are useful because they help without pretending certainty.

When you know the concept

“I can explain what that term means. Then let's check how it applies to your university and major.”

When you need to verify

“I don't want to answer that from memory because the academic year and destination matter. Let's look at the current source.”

When it becomes individualized advising

“I can help you understand the system, but I don't want to guess about your whole transfer plan. This is a good one to take to counseling with the source we just found.”

Who owns which question?

Referral is more useful when it is specific.

Counselor / advisor

Whole education plan, sequencing, transcript-wide decisions, GE/major/pathway integration, catalog-rights questions, and individualized transfer planning.

Articulation officer

How institutional courses articulate, questions about ASSIST agreement interpretation or missing articulation, and faculty curriculum changes that may affect articulation.

Receiving university admissions / program

Admission eligibility, program selection, impaction/selectivity, conditions of admission, change-of-major rules, application-specific questions, and policies owned by that institution.

Financial aid office

FAFSA/CADAA issues, satisfactory academic progress, aid eligibility, special-circumstance review, award questions, and financial consequences of enrollment decisions.

A useful faculty move: when you refer, send the student with a specific question and—when possible—the official page you were looking at. “Go see counseling” is much more useful when it becomes “Take this ASSIST major agreement to counseling and ask which of these preparation courses should come first in your plan.”
