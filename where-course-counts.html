Where Does My Course Count?

“Does my course count?” is not one question. Start with what you are trying to prove, then use the source that actually answers that question.

One course can have several different statuses.

None of these automatically proves all the others.

The source map

Use the source that owns the claim.

A common faculty mistake is finding one favorable status and stretching it into a claim the source never made.

ASSIST list CSU / UC Transferable Courses

Best for: “Will the system award transfer unit credit?” Review the important information at the top of the list and use the correct academic year.

ASSIST list Cal-GETC / GE / admissions lists

Best for: approval in the listed area and effective terms. GE status is not a substitute for checking major preparation.

ASSIST agreement Department / Prefix / course articulation

Best for: official relationships between a CCC course and the receiving institution's coursework or requirements.

ASSIST agreement View Agreement by Major

Best for: whether CCC coursework can satisfy lower-division preparation for the named major at that institution.

C-ID Approved course / descriptor search

Best for: verifying whether the local course has been approved against a C-ID descriptor and for reviewing current descriptor/TMC information.

CCCCO + local COR Common Course Numbering

Best for: confirming the statewide CCN template and how the locally approved course aligns with the current common-numbered course requirements.

C-ID TMC + local curriculum ADT / Transfer Model Curriculum

Best for: understanding the statewide model and then verifying how the local college's approved ADT actually uses the course.

ASSIST or receiving institution AICCU / independent institutions

Participating AICCU institutions can publish agreements in ASSIST. If the institution does not participate, use the receiving university's official transfer-credit information.

What did you actually prove?

A positive result has boundaries.

“It is UC transferable.”

You may say: UC awards transfer unit credit for the course under the applicable list/year.

You may not automatically say: it satisfies Cal-GETC, a specific UC course, or the student's major preparation.

“It is approved for Cal-GETC Area X.”

You may say: the course is approved for the listed area during the applicable effective terms.

You may not automatically say: every student should take it or that it satisfies preparation for a specific major.

“It has a C-ID designation.”

You may say: the course was approved against the specified C-ID descriptor.

You may not automatically say: it is articulated identically to every CSU/UC course or satisfies every major requirement.

“It has a CCN number.”

You may say: it is part of the statewide common-numbering framework and should be evaluated against the current CCN template/local COR.

You still check separately: GE, university-specific articulation, major preparation, and local program roles.

“ASSIST shows it in the major agreement.”

You may say: the agreement identifies how the course can satisfy the named major's lower-division preparation for that institution/year.

You still read: header notes, conditions, required/recommended language, grade requirements, and the whole agreement.

“No course articulated.”

Do not translate this into: “our course does not transfer.”

It means no established articulation is displayed for that item in that agreement. Transferability is a separate question.

Build a course passport

Know the transfer roles your course actually has.

This is not a score. A course does not need every box checked. The goal is to replace vague institutional memory with a current profile.

What to record beside the boxes

  • Academic year / effective term. Status can change.
  • Source link. Make the next check easy.
  • Destinations checked. “Articulated” is not universal.
  • Notes / conditions. Do not reduce an agreement to a green checkmark.
  • Last reviewed date. Institutional memory needs an expiration date.

Useful department practice: keep this as a lightweight shared course profile rather than asking every faculty member to rediscover the same information independently.

Questions that cause trouble

Open these before making the easy assumption.

Our course has the same CCN number. Does that mean articulation is automatic everywhere?

No. CCN improves statewide comparability, numbering, and mobility, but a faculty member should still verify the actual GE, major-preparation, and university-specific articulation claims being made.

Our course has C-ID. Why do I still need ASSIST?

C-ID verifies alignment to a statewide descriptor. ASSIST answers institution-pair and agreement questions. When the claim is “this satisfies requirement X at University Y,” use the source that actually publishes that relationship.

The course has been in Cal-GETC before. Can I just call it Cal-GETC approved?

Check the current ASSIST list and effective terms. ASSIST specifically publishes effective begin/end information for Cal-GETC lists. A historical approval does not justify an undated claim forever.

We changed the COR but kept the same course number. Are the old transfer roles safe?

Do not assume that. Substantive local curriculum changes can intersect with articulation, C-ID, CCN template requirements, GE, and program use. Bring the articulation officer into the review early.

If the course is changing

The course passport becomes your pre-revision checklist.

Before approving a transfer-relevant revision, identify every current role that could be affected.

1. Capture today

Document current transferability, GE, articulation, C-ID/CCN, and program roles before changing the COR.

2. Compare the revision

Which content, objectives, units, methods, texts, prerequisites, or other elements are changing?

3. Ask articulation early

Identify which approvals or agreements may need review, resubmission, notification, or monitoring.

4. Update the passport

After approval, replace old assumptions with the new effective status and review date.
