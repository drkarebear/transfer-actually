The general-education part of transfer

Cal-GETC, Actually.

Cal-GETC can take care of a large piece of lower-division general education before transfer. It is important—but it is only one piece of the transfer plan.

The one-sentence version

Cal-GETC is California's single lower-division general-education transfer pattern for students newly enrolling at a California Community College beginning in fall 2025.

Cal-GETC = general education. It does not automatically equal major prep, admission, or a complete transfer plan.

First distinction

What does Cal-GETC actually do?

It solves a general-education problem. It does not solve every transfer problem.

It can do this

Cover lower-division GE

For CSU transfer, certified Cal-GETC coursework satisfies the designated lower-division GE areas. For UC transfer, Cal-GETC can satisfy most freshman/sophomore-level GE requirements.

It does not automatically do this

Finish major preparation

A student may still need specific courses for a major, especially in selective or high-unit fields.

It does not automatically do this

Guarantee admission

Cal-GETC itself is not a UC admission requirement and is not a guarantee of admission to a CSU or UC campus or major.

It does not automatically do this

Finish every graduation requirement

Universities can have upper-division GE and other graduation requirements after transfer. UC language requirements can also still matter in some colleges and majors.

The memory trick: Think GE first. Whenever a question is about a major, admission, a specific campus, or graduation, you have moved beyond what Cal-GETC alone can answer.

The pattern

Six areas. Eleven courses. Thirty-four semester units.

These are the statewide subject areas. The actual courses that satisfy them depend on the Cal-GETC approvals at the college where the course was taken.

1

English Communication

English composition, critical thinking and composition, and oral communication. 3 courses / 9 semester units.

2

Mathematical Concepts & Quantitative Reasoning

1 course / 3 semester units.

3

Arts & Humanities

2 courses, including at least one arts and one humanities course / 6 semester units.

4

Social & Behavioral Sciences

2 courses from at least two disciplines / 6 semester units.

5

Physical & Biological Sciences

Physical science + biological science, with at least one laboratory / 7 semester units.

6

Ethnic Studies

1 course / 3 semester units.

Total: 11 courses and 34 semester units. A course must be approved for the Cal-GETC area at the college where it was completed, and minimum grade rules apply.

The big learning moment

General education is not the same thing as major preparation.

This distinction prevents a lot of transfer confusion.

Cal-GETC / General Education

  • Broad learning across disciplines
  • Can travel across CSU and UC transfer pathways
  • Has statewide subject areas
  • Can be certified by the community college
≠

Major Preparation

  • Courses specifically tied to the intended major
  • Can differ by university and major
  • May be central to admission selection
  • Should be checked in current articulation and campus guidance

Example: A biology student could finish Cal-GETC and still be missing chemistry, calculus, physics, or biology sequences required or recommended for the intended university program. The major decides what else matters.

How to use it

Use a four-step Cal-GETC habit.

1. Identify your pattern.

New CCC students beginning fall 2025 use Cal-GETC. Earlier students may have catalog-rights options to verify.

2. Check the local course list.

A course counts in the Cal-GETC area for which it was approved at the college where it was taken.

3. Check major prep separately.

Use ASSIST and current campus/major information instead of assuming GE completion covers the major.

4. Get certification handled.

Before transfer, work with the community college to make sure the appropriate Cal-GETC certification is sent when required.

Common myths

Four sentences that need a tiny rewrite.

“I finished Cal-GETC, so I'm ready to transfer.”

Maybe—but Cal-GETC only answers the GE part.

Better: I finished my lower-division GE pattern; now I need to verify transferable units, major preparation, admission requirements, and deadlines.

“If a course is transferable, it must count for Cal-GETC.”

No. Transferability and Cal-GETC approval are different questions.

Better: Check whether the course is specifically approved in the Cal-GETC area at the college where it was completed.

“Cal-GETC is required to apply to UC.”

UC says Cal-GETC is not an admission requirement.

Better: Cal-GETC can be a useful GE pathway, but major preparation may deserve priority for some majors and campuses.

“LOTE disappeared, so language never matters for UC anymore.”

Language Other Than English is not a Cal-GETC area, but it can still be a UC graduation requirement in some colleges and majors.

Better: Check the intended UC program's language expectations; UC currently provides optional LOTE-certification guidance during the Cal-GETC transition.

The nuances worth knowing.

I'm new to a CCC in fall 2025 or later. Which GE pattern do I use?

Cal-GETC is the singular CCC transfer GE pattern available to students beginning at a California Community College in fall 2025 or later.

I started before fall 2025. Do I automatically switch to Cal-GETC?

Not necessarily. CSU guidance tells continuously enrolled students to consult their institution about catalog rights, and UC guidance preserves IGETC for eligible students who matriculated before fall 2025. This is a “verify your own record” question.

Can I use courses from more than one community college?

Yes, but the course must be applied according to the Cal-GETC approval at the college where the course was completed. This is why “my other college said it counted” is not enough by itself.

What grade do I need?

UC's current Cal-GETC guidance states that courses count with grades of C or better; Credit/Pass can count when the community college treats that mark as equivalent to C or better. Always verify your college's current policy and any program-specific requirements.

Does Cal-GETC finish GE forever?

No. It addresses lower-division GE. CSU transfer students with certified Cal-GETC still complete upper-division GE after transfer, and universities may have other graduation requirements.

What should I do with this?

Student and faculty takeaways.

Student

Track GE and major prep as two separate lists.

That one habit makes transfer planning much easier. Use your college's Cal-GETC list for GE, then use ASSIST/current university guidance for the major.

Faculty

Explain the distinction, then point to verification.

You can help a student understand what GE means and why Cal-GETC is not the whole pathway. Do not guess whether a particular course, pattern, or catalog right applies to that student's record.
