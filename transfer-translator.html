UC + CSU Translator

You do not need to memorize every transfer acronym. Learn what the term does, when you might see it, and what it does not automatically mean.

Three UC terms that sound almost identical

UC TAP = planning toolTAG = admission guaranteeTAU = post-application academic update

They are connected, but they happen at different points in the transfer process.

UC

University of California

UC terms worth knowing

UC TAP

Transfer Admission Planner

UC's online planning tool for entering completed and planned coursework and tracking progress toward transfer requirements. Students also submit a TAG through UC TAP.

Why you care: TAP is a planner. It is not the UC admission application.

TAG

Transfer Admission Guarantee

A separate guarantee available to eligible California Community College students at one of six participating campuses: Davis, Irvine, Merced, Riverside, Santa Barbara, or Santa Cruz. Campus and major requirements vary.

Why you care: A TAG student still submits the regular UC application and must keep meeting the TAG conditions.

TAU

Transfer Academic Update

The required post-application update where transfer applicants report final fall grades and in-progress or planned coursework. UC says applicants must complete it even if nothing changed.

Why you care: A submitted application is not the end of the UC process.

UC Transfer Pathways

Common lower-division major preparation for selected UC majors. The goal is to prepare for the same major across multiple UC campuses rather than building an entirely different preparation plan for each one.

Why you care: A Pathway is major preparation. It is not itself an admission guarantee.

Pathways+

A route for eligible students whose major is covered by UC Transfer Pathways and who also want a TAG at a participating campus.

Why you care: Think Pathway preparation + TAG, not a third separate guarantee program.

PIQ

Personal Insight Question

Short UC application responses that help provide context about the applicant's experiences, preparation, interests, accomplishments, and thinking. Transfer applicants answer one required question plus three of seven optional questions.

Why you care: PIQs are not generic admissions essays. See PIQs, Actually.

SIR

Statement of Intent to Register

The step an admitted student uses to accept an offer of admission from a UC campus by the published deadline.

Why you care: Admission does not automatically become enrollment. The student still has to accept the offer and complete campus next steps.

Comprehensive review

UC's process for selecting among qualified applicants when a campus has more applicants than it can admit. Transfer review can consider preparation for the major, transferable GPA, honors, achievements, context, and other published factors.

Why you care: Meeting minimum transfer requirements does not guarantee admission to a selective campus or major.

Seven-course pattern

A UC minimum transfer-admission course pattern. It is one part of UC eligibility and should not be confused with Cal-GETC or major preparation.

Why you care: One course can serve more than one purpose, but the requirements still answer different questions.

UC-transferable

A course approved for UC transfer unit credit.

Why you care: UC-transferable does not automatically mean Cal-GETC, course-to-course articulation, or major preparation.

CSU

California State University

CSU terms worth knowing

ADT

Associate Degree for Transfer: AA-T or AS-T

A California Community College transfer degree that provides CSU-system admission benefits and, when the student enters a CSU program deemed similar to the ADT, degree-completion protections.

Why you care: The statewide ADT benefit is not automatically a promise of one particular CSU campus or major.

Similar major

A CSU bachelor's program officially determined to be similar to the student's ADT.

Why you care: The ADT's 60-unit-after-transfer protection is tied to admission into a program deemed similar and to the CSU's conditions.

TSP

Transfer Success Pathway

A separate agreement for eligible new California Community College students that can guarantee admission to the CSU campus/program in the agreement when its requirements are met.

Why you care: TSP is not another name for an ADT.

CSU Transfer Planner

An online CSU planning tool for California Community College students to explore CSU options and plan transfer progress.

Why you care: It helps organize the path; it does not replace Cal State Apply when it is time to apply.

Impaction

A CSU campus, applicant level, or major can be impacted when demand exceeds available capacity. Impacted programs or campuses can use additional admission criteria.

Why you care: Meeting CSU minimum eligibility may not be enough for an impacted choice.

Local admission area

A geographic relationship used by some CSU campuses in admission. Students from the campus's local service/admission area can receive priority compared with students from outside the area, especially where capacity is limited.

Why you care: Geography can affect selectivity at some CSU campuses.

UDT

Upper-Division Transfer

A CSU transfer applicant who will have at least 60 transferable semester units or 90 transferable quarter units by enrollment.

Why you care: Most CCC students plan for upper-division transfer, and the admission requirements differ from lower-division transfer.

LDT

Lower-Division Transfer

A CSU transfer applicant with 59 or fewer transferable semester units or 89 or fewer transferable quarter units by enrollment. Not every CSU accepts lower-division transfers.

Why you care: Fewer than 60 units can change which campuses and requirements apply.

Academic History Update

Sometimes shortened to AHU

The January Cal State Apply period when transfer applicants can update fall grades, planned spring coursework, colleges attended, and other academic information.

Why you care: Campus use varies. Some require it, some treat it as optional, and some do not consider it.

Program Materials

A section in Cal State Apply where a particular CSU program may collect program-specific prerequisites, questions, documents, or other information.

Why you care: Finishing the general application does not guarantee every program-specific requirement is complete.

Golden Four

The CSU transfer-admission courses in written communication, critical thinking, oral communication, and mathematical concepts/quantitative reasoning.

Why you care: They are admission requirements with timing and grade rules; do not treat them as just four random GE courses.

Redirection

A CSU process that can send an eligible application to another CSU with capacity when the original choice could not admit the student because of space or impaction.

Why you care: A denial from the first-choice campus is not always the end of the CSU path.

Both systems

Shared transfer language

ASSIST

California's official statewide source for articulation and transferability information among California public institutions and participating independent institutions.

Use it for: transferability lists, GE lists, course articulation, and major agreements.

Cal-GETC

California's current intersegmental lower-division general education pattern for students newly enrolling at a CCC beginning fall 2025, subject to catalog-rights rules for earlier students.

Remember: GE completion is not the same as major-preparation completion.

Major preparation

Lower-division coursework expected for a particular major/program before transfer.

Remember: the same major name can have different preparation at different campuses.

Articulation

An official relationship explaining how coursework at one institution can satisfy coursework or requirements at another.

Remember: “no course articulated” is not the same as “not transferable.”

Best habit: when an acronym starts driving the plan, open the current official source. The glossary explains the term; it does not turn a general definition into an individualized transfer decision.
