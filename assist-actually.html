Use the tool without becoming the tool

ASSIST, Actually.

ASSIST is where California transfer questions get specific. The trick is knowing what you are trying to find before you open it.

The one idea to remember

ASSIST is a verification tool, not a complete transfer plan.

Know the question → choose the right agreement → read the notes → verify the year

Start with your question

What are you trying to find?

Choose the closest task. This tells you what kind of ASSIST information to look for.

The four moves

Almost every ASSIST search starts the same way.

The official ASSIST guidance begins with the academic year, then the sending institution, then the receiving institution. After that, you choose the agreement or list that matches your question.

1

Choose the academic year.

The year matters. Do not silently assume the newest agreement answers an older catalog-year question—or vice versa.

2

Choose where the credit comes from.

This is the sending institution: usually the California Community College where the course was completed.

3

Choose where the credit is going.

This is the receiving institution: the CSU, UC, or participating independent institution you want to check.

4

Choose the right view.

Major, department, prefix, GE/breadth, or another available list. Greyed-out options simply are not available for that search.

No agreement for that year? ASSIST may point you to the closest available academic year. Treat that as information to investigate—not permission to assume the older or newer agreement automatically applies.

1. Read the header and notes. Requirements, conditions, alternatives, and warnings often live above the course rows.
2. Keep left and right straight. One side is the sending institution; the other is the receiving institution. Do not mentally reverse them.
3. “No course articulated” is specific. It tells you established articulation is not shown for that relationship. It does not automatically mean the CCC course is nontransferable or useless.
4. Check the academic year again. Before acting on the agreement, make sure the year shown is the year relevant to the question you are answering.

Choose the right lens

Major, department, prefix… why so many views?

Different views answer different questions. If a view is unavailable, ASSIST greys it out rather than inventing an agreement.

By major

Best when the question is: “What preparation does this university major identify from this sending institution?”

By department or prefix

Useful when you want to inspect course-to-course or grouped articulation within a disciplinary area.

Transferability / GE lists

Useful when the question is about CSU or UC transfer unit credit, Cal-GETC, CSU AI, or another published list rather than one major.

Newer useful feature: agreement comparison. ASSIST now offers an Agreement Comparison tool that can display two articulation agreements side by side. That is especially useful when you want to compare pathways rather than toggling between screens from memory.

Important boundary

ASSIST does a lot. It does not do everything.

Knowing what ASSIST does not answer is part of using it well.

Not a complete admissions guide Campus admission requirements, GPA rules, application procedures, deadlines, and other admissions information belong in current CSU, UC, or institutional admissions resources.
Not an individualized educational plan ASSIST can show articulation and transferability information. It does not know a student's entire record, goals, catalog rights, or personal circumstances.
Not an out-of-state articulation database ASSIST's student guidance says it does not currently include campuses outside California.
Not a reason to ignore the notes Important conditions can appear in headers, footnotes, and introductory text. A course row without its notes can be misleading.
