A transfer-aware curriculum workflow

We're Revising a Course. What Should We Check?

Local curriculum belongs to faculty. Transfer consequences, however, can extend beyond the local COR. Before changing a transfer-relevant course, capture what the course does today and identify which outside approvals or agreements deserve another look.

The safest sequence

Do not wait until after curriculum approval to discover that a course was carrying more transfer infrastructure than anyone remembered.

1. Capture current roles 2. Identify the change 3. Flag systems to recheck 4. Involve articulation early 5. Publish + verify the new status

Before editing the COR

Take a snapshot of the course as it exists today.

This gives the department something concrete to protect, reconsider, or intentionally change.

Capture the current transfer passport.

  • CSU and UC transferability
  • Cal-GETC / other applicable GE approval
  • Course-to-course and department articulation
  • Major-preparation agreements
  • C-ID designation and current descriptor
  • CCN status and current template, if applicable
  • ADT/TMC and local degree/pathway use

Build the course passport in “Where Does My Course Count?”

Describe the proposed change precisely.

  • What is changing in the COR?
  • What is staying identical?
  • What effective term is intended?
  • Is this a CCN course with template-controlled elements?
  • Is the current C-ID approval based on a COR that will no longer be the active version?
  • Do students currently rely on this course in an ADT or major sequence?

Useful habit: compare old COR → proposed COR, not memory → proposed COR.

The downstream systems

Different systems care about different parts of the COR.

That is why one generic “substantive change” rule is not enough.

Explicit UC TCA triggers

UC transferability / TCA

UC's current TCA guidance says new courses and significant/substantive revisions should be submitted for TCA review. It specifically identifies changes to:

  • course content
  • course objectives
  • prerequisites/corequisites
  • a decrease in units
  • lecture hours changing to lab hours or vice versa

UC separately lists title, prefix, number, textbook updates, unit increases, and cross-listing as technical changes that do not require TCA re-review.

Check C-ID approval

C-ID

C-ID has published guidance treating significant local changes—including content, objectives/SLOs, prerequisites/corequisites, and a unit change below the descriptor minimum—as changes that require a new COR review.

Faculty move: if the active COR is changing in one of these ways, do not assume the old C-ID approval automatically describes the revised course. Verify the current C-ID process with the articulation officer/C-ID.

Template-controlled course

Common Course Numbering

CCCCO's current CCN templates contain required taxonomy and identical language in specified COR elements, including description, objectives/outcomes, content, methods of evaluation, and representative texts.

The Chancellor's Office explicitly tells curriculum committees to work with articulation on common-numbered courses and consider whether added local content could affect course-to-course or major-preparation articulation.

Destination-specific check

Course + major articulation

Even when a change does not automatically trigger a systemwide UC TCA review, it may matter to campus-specific course-to-course or major-preparation articulation.

Faculty move: ask the articulation officer which published agreements currently depend on the course and whether the receiving institutions need updated information.

Approval-specific check

Cal-GETC / GE / admissions areas

Do not infer continued GE status solely because the course kept the same number. When a transfer-relevant COR changes, verify whether the applicable GE approval remains valid or requires review under the current process.

Record the effective term, not just “GE approved.”

Program ripple

ADT / TMC / local degrees

A revised course may still be listed in an ADT, TMC-based option, local degree, prerequisite chain, program map, or recommended pathway.

Faculty move: identify every program that uses the course before finalizing a change that could alter units, prerequisites, sequence, or descriptor alignment.

The curriculum workflow

Transfer-aware does not mean transfer controls the curriculum.

It means the consequences are visible early enough for faculty to make informed choices.

1

Snapshot

Document current transfer, articulation, C-ID/CCN, and program roles.

2

Draft

Identify the exact COR changes and intended effective term.

3

Consult

Ask articulation/curriculum which external approvals or agreements may be affected.

4

Approve

Complete the local curriculum process with those consequences understood.

5

Re-verify

After approval, track the revised status rather than continuing to publish the old course passport.

Important distinction: “This may affect articulation” is not the same as “faculty cannot make the change.” The purpose of the check is informed curricular decision-making and accurate downstream maintenance.

After local approval

The effective term matters in ASSIST.

ASSIST's curriculum-management guidance says updates should be made in the term in which the curriculum change becomes effective, and unpublished updates do not appear on assist.org or become available for downstream articulation work.

Post-approval checklist

Do not let the old transfer story survive the new COR.

Confirm the effective term.

  • When does the revised COR become active?
  • Which students/catalog materials will see the old vs. new version?
  • Does the date line up with ASSIST curriculum history?

Publish/update the course data.

  • Articulation/data management updates the correct ASSIST term.
  • Confirm the revised course information is actually published.
  • Keep a source link or status note rather than relying on email memory.

Track outside review when needed.

  • UC TCA re-review
  • C-ID resubmission/review
  • GE/Cal-GETC review as applicable
  • Receiving-institution articulation updates
  • CCN template compliance

Update local student-facing pathways.

  • ADT / degree requirements
  • Program maps and sequencing
  • Department webpages and advising materials
  • Faculty transfer guidance
  • The course passport / last-reviewed date

Common questions

A few distinctions worth keeping visible.

We're only changing the title or course number. Is that automatically safe?

For UC TCA re-review, title, prefix, and number changes are currently listed as technical changes that do not require TCA review. But the change still needs accurate local/ASSIST implementation, and a CCN course's number/title structure cannot be treated as a purely local choice. Check the systems that actually apply to the course.

We're only updating textbooks. Do we need UC TCA re-review?

UC currently lists textbook updates as a technical change that does not require TCA re-review. That does not mean texts are irrelevant to every other process: current UC outline guidance has textbook expectations for course submissions, and CCN templates can control representative-text language. Use the rule for the specific system you are maintaining.

We're changing prerequisites only because the prerequisite got a new CCN number.

UC's current TCA guidance includes a specific exception: if the only prerequisite/corequisite change is the department and number—for example, an old local English number changing to ENGL C1000—the course does not need TCA re-review for that reason alone. Still implement the change accurately in the local COR and ASSIST.

Can local faculty add content to a CCN course?

CCCO says the templates establish required identical elements and specifically directs curriculum committees to work with articulation when considering additional local content. The question is not simply “are local additions allowed?” but whether the proposed COR still satisfies the template and whether the addition could affect course-to-course or major-preparation articulation.

Who should initiate all these checks?

The exact local workflow varies. A practical model is: discipline faculty identify the proposed academic change; curriculum staff/committee manage local process; the articulation officer identifies articulation/transfer-review consequences; program faculty and counseling help surface degree/pathway implications. The important thing is that these conversations happen before downstream problems become surprises.
