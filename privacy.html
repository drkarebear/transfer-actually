Privacy without the surveillance stack

Privacy

Transfer information should not require you to hand over personal information. Transfer, Actually is intentionally designed to do its job without building a visitor profile.

What Transfer, Actually does not collect

Transfer, Actually does not ask for your name, email address, student ID, transcript, login credentials, or other personal information. The site does not use its own analytics trackers, advertising trackers, third-party embeds, or cookies.

Your answers stay in your browser

Quizzes, checklists, and decision tools run locally in the page. Your selections are not submitted to Transfer, Actually, saved in a database, or used to build a profile. They reset when the page is reloaded or closed.

Please do not enter or send sensitive information. Transfer, Actually does not need transcripts, student ID numbers, passwords, Social Security numbers, financial-aid records, education records, or other confidential information to explain transfer concepts.

When you follow a link to another website

Transfer, Actually links to official resources such as ASSIST, UC, CSU, CCCCO, and C-ID. When you follow one of those links, you leave Transfer, Actually and the destination site's own privacy practices apply.

This site sets a no-referrer browser policy so the destination does not need to receive the address of the Transfer, Actually page you came from.

Hosting and ordinary technical logs

The service that hosts the site may process ordinary technical request information needed to deliver webpages, such as an IP address, browser information, timestamps, and requested files. Transfer, Actually does not add its own visitor-tracking layer on top of the hosting service.

No student-record system

Transfer, Actually is an educational information site, not a counseling record, admissions system, learning-management system, or FERPA record repository. Do not use it as a place to store or transmit student records.

Changes to this privacy approach

If the site later adds a feature that collects or transmits information—such as a feedback form or analytics—the privacy page should be updated before that feature is published. The current design preference is to avoid collecting personal information unless a future feature truly requires it.
